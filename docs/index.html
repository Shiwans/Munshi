The problem

The khata is still a paper diary and a chat with yourself

In Dadar's wholesale market, a tomato trader sells to dozens of hotels and vendors a day, mostly on udhaar. Every sale goes into a red diary in their own shorthand, payments go in a side column, and the real balance lives in their head. Bookkeeping apps exist, but they want forms, English and a separate screen. Nobody fills forms at 6 am with crates moving.

"Diary mein likh deta hoon, baad mein hisaab karta hoon. Kabhi kabhi baad aata hi nahi."

  • Udhaar is forgotten or disputed: "maine to 1500 diye the"
  • End-of-day totals take an hour with a calculator
  • Customers ask for a statement and get a photo of a messy page
  • Apps that ask for typing in forms get abandoned in a week

Why a trader can trust it

Five promises Munshi keeps

✓

The AI never does the maths

The model only reads what is written. Every total and balance is calculated by plain code, to the paisa, and the working is shown: 72 kg × ₹28 = ₹2,016.

✓

Nothing is saved without your haan

Every entry, photo row and voice note comes back as a card first. New customers are marked "(naya customer)" so a misspelt name never quietly becomes a new account.

✓

It checks your diary's sums too

If the page says 3,555 but the quantities add up to 3,195, Munshi asks which is right instead of guessing. Unreadable amounts are flagged, never invented.

✓

Nothing is ever erased

Mistakes are cancelled with a reversing entry, so the history stays honest. Accidentally deleted everything? /wapas brings it back. A dated backup is kept every day.

✓

Your khata stays yours

Each trader sees only their own data. Voice notes are transcribed on the laptop. In local mode, nothing at all leaves the machine. Off-topic messages and attempts to trick the AI are stopped before any model sees them.

Built on open models and open tools
